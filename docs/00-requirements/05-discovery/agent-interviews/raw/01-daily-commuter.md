# Discovery interview — Daily commuter

Scenario probes removed; follow-ups chosen by the persona.

**Simulated by:** Claude subagent (discovery method, no system described) · **Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Persona:** Daily commuter on a suburban line, same morning and evening train five days a week, phone-checker, non-technical.

## Who I am
I take the same train in the morning and the same one home, five days a week, and I have for years. I know my platform and roughly how the line behaves. I check my phone while walking to the station, one hand, squinting in the sun, and I give it a few seconds. I'm not technical; I just want to know whether to hurry or relax.

## Answers

### Q1
I leave home at about the same time every day and look at my phone on the walk. I want one answer: is my train on time, and which platform. Usually I already know the platform, so I mostly look for anything that is different today. At the station I glance at the board and listen for announcements. I get on, and if it is crowded I stand. In the evening it is the same in reverse, but I am more tired and more annoyed by delays.

### Q2
Delays, cancellations, and platform changes are the big ones. A cancellation is the worst because I find out late, often only at the platform. When it breaks I look for the next train, check whether the later one will be packed, and sometimes message my boss that I'll be late. Sometimes I give up and find a bus or a lift. A platform change announced at the last minute means running over a footbridge. I'm guessing at the causes; I only see the result.

### Q3
I need the train time, whether it is running late and by how much, whether it is cancelled, and the platform. I get that from the phone and the station boards, plus announcements. I need it before I leave the house and again while walking, because that is when I can still change my plan. Once I'm on the platform it is too late for most options. I also want to know if the information is current, not an hour old.

### Q4
I never want to be told the train is fine when it has gone, or to stand on the wrong platform because something was out of date. I never want to be charged for something I did not get. I also don't want to have to dig through lots of screens with one hand in the sun. Surprises at the last second are what I hate.

### Q5
If what I was told was confidently wrong, I'd find out when I got to the station and the board disagreed. I'd believe the station board and the announcement over my phone. After one or two of those I would stop trusting the phone and go back to just turning up early. If it said nothing was wrong and it was, I'd be angry; if it said "I'm not sure," I'd at least be careful.

## Follow-ups

### Follow-up 1 — Finding out about a cancellation only at the platform (cost: time and money)
The last time was a few weeks ago, a weekday morning. On the walk my phone showed my train as normal, nothing odd. I got to the platform about six minutes early and the board still listed it, but people were bunched up looking at each other. Then an announcement said it was cancelled. I asked a staff member in a vest; he said he'd only just been told and the next one was "in about twenty minutes", though I'm guessing he wasn't sure. I checked the phone again and it only changed to cancelled a couple of minutes after the announcement. I messaged my boss, then waited, and the next train was packed so I stood the whole way. I got in about forty minutes late. I'd paid for a monthly ticket, so nothing came back; I just lost the time. It ended with me annoyed and leaving earlier for a week.

### Follow-up 2 — The phone disagreeing with the board (cost: trust)
This was maybe two months ago, an evening. The phone said my train was on time from platform 2. I'm used to platform 2, so I went straight over without looking up. On the way down the stairs I heard an announcement saying it was now platform 4. I had to turn around, climb the footbridge and run. I made the train, but only just, and someone near me missed it. Nobody contacted me; I only knew because I happened to hear it. Afterwards I checked the phone on the train and it still showed platform 2, so it was out of date by several minutes, I'm guessing, at least. It ended without harm to me, but since then I always look at the board first and the phone second.

## Extracted by the persona
- **What I rely on today**: phone for status, station boards, announcements, staff in vests, other commuters, my own memory of the route.
- **What is hardest or most error-prone in my work**: late discovery of cancellations and platform changes, and the phone lagging behind the board and announcements.
- **What must never happen**: being told the train is fine when it is not; standing on the wrong platform; paying for a trip that did not happen with no refund.
- **What would make me stop trusting information**: it disagrees with the station board even once or twice; it changes only after the announcement; no sign of how fresh it is; "late" with no figure.
- **What nobody gives me today that I would want**: an early, clear warning on the walk to the station; an honest "not sure / out of date" signal; some compensation or refund when a train is cancelled, even for a monthly ticket (I'm guessing how that would work).
- **Confidence**: Medium. I know my own routine well, but I'm guessing about causes and about how data, refunds and staff communication work behind the scenes.
