# Discovery interview — Occasional traveller

Scenario probes removed; follow-ups chosen by the persona.

**Simulated by:** Claude subagent (discovery method, no system described) · **Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Persona:** Occasional rail passenger, a few trips a year, unfamiliar with the station and network, travelling with luggage, anxious, possibly little or no mobile data and a phone set to another language.

## Who I am
I take the train maybe three or four times a year, usually to a city I have only visited twice. I carry a suitcase and a bag, so I cannot move quickly. I do not know the stations or how the network is laid out. I am always afraid I will miss my train, so I arrive early and check everything several times.

## Answers
### Q1 Normal day
I check my ticket and the departure time the night before, and again in the morning. I get to the station well early because I do not trust myself to find things. First I look for a big board showing departures and find my train and its platform, then I walk there with my luggage. If the platform is not shown yet, I stand near the board and wait. I listen for announcements, though I often cannot understand them well. Once on board I watch for station names to know when to get off. I am tense until I am sitting down.

### Q2 What breaks
The platform is not displayed until late, so I do not know where to go. Trains are delayed or cancelled and I find out only when I am already at the station. Announcements are fast, unclear, or in a language I do not fully follow. My phone data runs out or the signal is bad, and then I cannot check anything. When something breaks I ask staff if I can find one, or I ask other passengers, and I copy whatever they do. I am guessing here, but sometimes I board the wrong train because I trusted someone who looked sure.

### Q3 Information I rely on
I rely on the ticket, the departure board, announcements, and signs. I also use my phone for the time and sometimes a travel website. I need the platform and any delay about thirty minutes before departure, and again when I am walking to the platform. I need to know the next station on the train. I need information in a language I can read, with simple words. When I must change trains, I need it as soon as I buy the ticket and again before I get off.

### Q4 Never see or happen
I never want to be told the wrong platform and watch my train leave. I never want to be stranded at night with no information. I never want to be left without a reason when something is delayed. I do not want a screen that disagrees with the announcement and gives no hint which one is right. I do not want to feel I am the only person who did not get told.

### Q5 Confidently wrong information
If a board or message was confidently wrong, I would probably believe it first, because I do not know the station and have nothing to compare it with. I would only start to doubt it if I saw other passengers going somewhere else. Then I would ask a staff member or another traveller. If I found it was wrong I would stop trusting that source and go back to asking people. I would also leave much more time in future. I would feel foolish, but I would blame the source, not myself.

## Follow-ups
### Follow-up 1 — The platform that appeared late (cost: time and near-miss)
The last time, I arrived forty minutes early at a big station. First I saw my train on the board with the platform column blank. I checked my ticket, then my phone, which had almost no data and would not load. I waited by the board and kept looking up. About ten minutes before departure a platform appeared, at the far end of the station. I had to drag my suitcase up a flight of stairs; I did not see a lift and did not know where one was. I got on with about two minutes left, out of breath. It ended fine, but I was shaking, and I now arrive even earlier. I'm guessing the platform was only decided late, but nobody told me that.

### Follow-up 2 — Following the wrong person (cost: money and safety)
Once, on a change at a station I did not know, I could not find my connecting train on the board. I asked a man on the platform who looked like he knew his way, and he said "this one" and pointed. I boarded. About twenty minutes later the stops sounded wrong, and I checked the ticket and asked a woman, who told me I was heading the wrong way. I got off at a small station with no staff and little signal. Waiting for a train back took about an hour and a half, and I paid for another ticket. I'm guessing he simply misread it. It ended with me arriving late in the evening, tired, and afraid of the next change.

## Extracted by the persona
- **What I rely on today**: ticket, departure boards, announcements, signs, staff, other passengers, sometimes a phone website when I have data.
- **What is hardest or most error-prone in my work**: finding the right platform in time with luggage, rushed or foreign-language announcements, and changing trains at an unknown station. When I cannot confirm, I trust strangers.
- **What must never happen**: sent to the wrong platform or the wrong train, missing a train because information came late, stranded with no explanation, and conflicting information with no way to tell which is right.
- **What would make me stop trusting information**: a platform or time that turns out wrong once, a board that disagrees with the announcement, or a delay shown too late.
- **What nobody gives me today that I would want**: early, clear notice of platform changes; walking time, stairs and lift for a change of train; a way to check I am on the right train that works without data and in my language; a sign of which source is the latest.
- **Confidence**: Medium. The experiences are plausible for an anxious first-time visitor, but I am a simulated person and I am guessing about how any real railway shows its information.
