# Interview — Occasional traveller (passenger)

**Simulated by:** Claude subagent · **Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Persona:** Passenger who rides a few times a year, to a city they have visited twice, with luggage; unfamiliar with the station and network; anxious about missing the train; may not have the app and may use a foreign-language phone.

## Who I am
I take the train three or four times a year, usually to visit family or for a short trip. I have been to the destination city twice, so I do not really know it. I always have a suitcase and a bag, which makes stairs and crowds stressful. I get to the station early because I am afraid of missing the train.

## Answers

### Q1. Walk me through a normal day in your role.
There is no normal day, which is the problem. I buy the ticket a few days before, or at the station if I have not got round to it. I arrive well early and look for a big board to find my train and its platform. Then I find the platform, usually with a suitcase and a lot of second-guessing. I stand and wait, watching the board over my head and checking my phone. When the train comes I want to be sure it is the right one before I board. Getting off at the other end I have to repeat the whole thing in a place I barely know.

### Q2. What breaks, and what do you do when it does?
The things that go wrong are a platform change at the last minute, a delay with no explanation, and a train that is not what I expected, such as one that skips my stop. When that happens I look around at other people and copy them, and I ask staff if I can find any. If nobody is there, I panic a bit and pick the most likely platform. I have once got on the wrong train. I am guessing at how often this happens to others, but it is the thing I fear most. Announcements are often unclear to me, and in a crowd I cannot hear them.

### Q3. What do you need to see, and when?
Before I leave home I need the departure time and whether it is running. At the station I need the platform, and I need to be told at once if it changes. I need to know how long I have to get there with a suitcase. On the train I need to know which stop is mine and when it is coming, because I do not recognise stations. Plain words and big text matter more to me than anything clever.

### Q4. What must you never see (or never happen)?
I must never be told a platform and then find the train is not there, with no warning. I never want two screens saying different things. I do not want a "delayed" label with no new time, or "on time" for a train that is clearly late. I never want to be charged for a ticket and have nothing to show for it. I do not want to be left unsure whether I can still board.

### Q5. If this system lied to you — showed something confidently wrong — what would you do?
I would believe it at first, and that is the danger. If a board said platform 3 and a train arrived on platform 5, I would hurry over and ask someone nearby, then trust that person over the board. After that I would trust the screens less and just watch the crowd. I would probably not report it, because I would be rushing to catch the train. If it happened twice, I would stop using the app and ask staff every time.

## Probes

### Probe 1. You are standing on platform 3 and the platform changes. How do you find out, and what goes wrong?
Honestly, I find out by noticing other people start walking away, or by hearing an announcement I may not follow. If the board over my head updates, I might not look up again, because I already checked it. With a suitcase I need extra time to reach another platform, so a late change can mean missing the train. If I have the app, I might get an alert, but only if I followed this train, and I am not sure I would have thought to. My phone may be in another language, so an alert could be hard to read. The worst case is a change that appears on the board but not on my phone, so I am left with two answers.

### Probe 2. You need to change trains at a station you do not know. What do you need to be told, and when?
Before the journey I need to know where I change and how long I have. Before we arrive, I need to be told in time to collect my bags and stand by the door. At the station I need to know which platform, and how to walk there, ideally whether there are stairs, because of the luggage. I need to know if the connection is at risk because my first train is late, and what to do if I miss it. I do not want to find out on the platform. I'm guessing a short change feels fine on paper but is hard in an unfamiliar station.

### Probe 3. You do not have the app and have no data. What do you do at the station, and what would help most?
I look for a big board and I walk up to anyone in uniform. If there is a terminal I would try it, but only if it is obvious how to use it and lets me look up my train without signing up for anything. I would not install an app on the spot with no data. What helps most is a clear board at the entrance and another at the platform, showing the same thing, in simple words. Someone at a desk who can tell me my platform would also help. Symbols and numbers work better for me than long sentences, since I may not read the local language well.

## Extracted by the persona
- **What I need**: Platform and time shown clearly, and changes pushed to me quickly; plain wording and large text; guidance for connections and for my stop; ways to get information without an app or data.
- **What must never happen**: A wrong platform shown with confidence; two sources contradicting each other; a delay with no new time; paying and having no ticket; being left unsure whether I can board.
- **What would make me stop trusting it**: One confidently wrong platform or time. After that I would only trust people and what I can see with my own eyes.
- **What this system seems to be missing**: Anything for people who are lost or non-native speakers; luggage and walking-time awareness; connection-at-risk warnings; a human fallback; an obvious route for people without the app.
- **Where I disagree with the design as described**: It assumes I will follow a train and carry a phone with data. Alerts only for followed trains do not help someone who does not know to follow one. Displays and terminals seem like extras, but for me they are the main way in.
- **Confidence**: Medium. It is based on common traveller experience, but I am guessing about how often things go wrong and how any real operator behaves.
