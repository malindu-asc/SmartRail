# Interview — Station Manager (busy interchange)

**Simulated by:** Claude subagent · **Date:** 2026-10-06 · **Persona:** Station manager of a busy interchange; runs platforms, gates, information staff, crowd safety and local facilities; does not decide whether trains run.

## Who I am
I run a big interchange station. I am responsible for the platforms, the gates, my information staff, crowd safety and the shops and facilities. I spend as much time on the concourse as in the office. Rush hour is the job. Control decides whether trains run; I deal with what that does to the people in my building.

## Answers

### Q1. A normal day
I start before the first train, walking the station: gates, lifts, toilets, displays, anything left overnight. Then I brief the early team on anything planned, such as engineering works or events. The morning peak is the hard part, because I'm watching platforms and barriers more than a screen. Between peaks I handle staffing, complaints, cleaning and contractors, and the odd lost child. The evening peak is the same again, and on event nights it is worse. I'm guessing at the exact split of my day, but this is how most managers I'd know work. I'm on the radio all day and my phone buzzes constantly.

### Q2. What breaks, and what I do
Things break all the time: trains late, platforms swapped, a display frozen, a lift out, a gate jammed, a big crowd after a cancellation. I send staff to where the crowd is and make announcements by voice. I call control to find out what is really happening. If it might become unsafe, I hold people back, close an entrance or meter the flow. Most of my work is telling people something that the screens haven't yet.

### Q3. What I need to see, and when
I need to know about changes before the public does, or at the same moment at worst, never after. I want to see every platform change, delay and cancellation for my station in one place, with what changed and when. I also want to see which of my own equipment is faulty, and where the crowd is building. Speed matters more than detail. Two minutes' warning of a platform change is worth more than a perfect explanation ten minutes later.

### Q4. What I must never see
I must never see passengers told one platform on the screen and another by my staff, or the app saying a train is running when I've been told it is cancelled. I must never find out about a problem in my station from a passenger or a photo on social media. I don't want a flood of minor alerts that bury the real ones, because then I stop looking. And I can't have anything that makes my staff stare at a screen while the platform needs them.

### Q5. If the system lied to me
I would stop trusting it quickly and go back to radio and phone. I would send staff to check the platform by eye, and I'd tell my team to ignore the screens and announce by voice. Then I'd report it to control and want it noted. Getting trust back takes weeks. One confident wrong platform at rush hour and my people will never believe it again.

### Probe 1. Platform change, two minutes out, 300 waiting
I need to know the moment control decides, not when the display updates. I need to be told the old and new platform, the departure time and which way the crowd must move. I'm guessing a minute is the most I can lose. First I send staff to the stairs and the new platform, then I make the announcement. Then I check that the displays and the passenger app agree with what I just said. If they don't, I have to know straight away, because people believe a screen over a voice. What worries me is the crowd crossing on a staircase, so I need to know too if the new platform is short or hard to reach.

### Probe 2. Dead platform display, nobody told me
I do not want to find out from a passenger. I want to be told, by the system, that a display has gone quiet or is showing old information, and which one. A blank screen is at least obvious. A screen that is frozen on old times is dangerous, because it looks fine. It should say plainly when it is out of date. When I'm told, I put a member of staff on that platform and call it in for repair.

### Probe 3. Cancellation and a crowd at the barriers
I want to know which train, how long until anything replaces it, and what passengers can do: next train, other routes, refunds. I want that before the crowd asks me. I also want to know how many people the cancelled train would have carried, though I'm guessing I'd only get an estimate. I put staff at the barriers and on the concourse, I announce, and I may stop letting people through to the platform. If the crowd is getting dense, my judgment on safety overrides everything, and I may call the police or the transport authority. I do not want to be the one telling people about refunds without knowing the rule.

## Extracted by the persona
- **What I need**: Platform changes, delays and cancellations for my station the instant control decides. One clear view of my station. Early warning that a display or other device is faulty or stale. Messages that match what my staff say. Something I can read in a few seconds while standing up.
- **What must never happen**: Screens, app and staff giving different platforms. A stale screen looking healthy. Finding out about a station problem from a passenger. Alert floods that hide the important ones.
- **What would make me stop trusting it**: One confident wrong platform at rush hour. Updates arriving after my own radio. Screens frozen without saying so.
- **What this system seems to be missing**: Anything for crowd safety or crowd size at the station. A way for me to tell the system my own local problems (a closed stair, a broken lift, a blocked gate). Any mention of my staff getting alerts on their phones. A clear rule on who announces on the loudspeaker. Device health for displays is not mentioned.
- **Where I disagree with the design as described**: The brief gives station information to displays and passengers, but not to the station manager. It says "does not replace safety procedures", which is right, but it leaves me with no tool for my main worry, the crowd. I do not want yet another console to watch on the concourse; it has to work on a phone.
- **Confidence**: Medium. I'm drawing on common station practice, but I'm guessing at specific timings, and I don't know this railway's rules or radio setup.
